#### Ch 1. Loading and Performance

> [!note]+ RPIC Responsibilities
> RPIC (Remote Pilot in Command) responsibilities:
>
> - Aircraft is properly loaded (prior to flying)
> - Verifies "weight and balance" condition
> - Must comply with manufacturer's W & B specs and restrictions (if available)

> [!note]+ Axes of Rotation
>
> - **Roll** (longitudinal) — _ailerons_
> - **Pitch** (lateral) — _elevators_
> - **Yaw** (vertical) — _rudder_

> [!info]+ Forces of Flight
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110155115.png]]
>
> Lift is from **WINGS (airfoil)** on fixed wing, propellers on rotorcraft.
>
> Applied at _Center of Pressure_ (also called Center of Lift).
>
> **Counteracts weight.**
>
> In straight-and-level flight, lift and weight are **EQUAL**.

> [!info]+ Principles of LIFT
> Angle of attack and relative wind interact with flight path.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110155642.png]]
>
> Perpendicular to the relative wind is **LIFT**.
>
> _Resultant Force_ = Lift vector plus drag vector.
>
> Lift is proportional to the **SPEED** of the aircraft and the **ANGLE OF ATTACK** of the airfoil.
>
> - Faster speed = more lift
> - Higher angle of attack = more lift
>
> Lift is a combination of two principles:
>
> - **Newton's Third Law** — "For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction."
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110160248.png]]
>
> - **Bernoulli's Principle** (biggest portion) — "When a pipe is restricted, speed increases, pressure decreases."
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110155103.png]]

> [!info]+ Weight
> Result of gravity.
>
> Applied at _**CENTER OF GRAVITY**_.
>
> Points to **CENTER OF THE EARTH**.

> [!info]+ THRUST
> "Horizontal Lift" — same principles; the propeller is an airfoil.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110162845.png]]

> [!info]+ DRAG
> **Parasite Drag** — byproduct of flying through the air.
>
> **INCREASES AS SPEED INCREASES**
>
> **Parasite Drag — 3 types:**
>
> - Form Drag — PT Cruiser vs. Ferrari
> - Interference Drag — 1+1 = 3 (drag is greater than the individual parts)
> - Skin Friction — sandpaper flies poorly
>
> **Lift-Induced Drag:**
>
> - Fixed wing — as soon as lift is created
> - Rotorcraft — as soon as lateral motion
>
> **Stall** — when airfoil reaches critical angle of attack, airflow separates and lift decreases, leading to nose dropping.

> [!danger]+ Load Factor
> _Load Factor_ — a representation of the acceleration of gravity, aka **RESULTANT LOAD**.
>
> - Increases with **BANK ANGLE** (during a coordinated, level turn).
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261111110813.png]]
>
> **THIS IS WHY THE BANK ANGLE IS IMPORTANT BECAUSE IT CAN ADD MULTIPLE G'S TO DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE AIRCRAFT.**
>
> _**STALL SPEED**_ INCREASES with load factor.
>
> **ABRUPT MANEUVERS** attempted during **SLOWER AIRSPEED** can lead to stalling since they can increase the load factor (which will increase the speed at which the aircraft is capable of stalling).
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261111112311.png]]

> [!info]+ Stability
> _Stability_ — the ability of an aircraft to **RETURN TO THE ORIGINAL FLIGHT PATH** after being disturbed.
>
> **Static Stability** — **INITIAL** tendency to go back to equilibrium (cup tilted on table); positive static stability.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261111130509.png]]
>
> **Dynamic Stability** — aircraft's **RESPONSE OVER TIME** when disturbed.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261111130704.png]]

> [!question]+  Exceeding CG Limits
>
> >
>
> _Envelope_ - an aircraft's aft and forward CG Limit
>
> If you load an airplane in such a way the CG is out of limit or OUTSIDE THE ENVELOPE, it won't perform properly
>
> - Too far **forward** : the airplane won't get off the ground
> - Too far **aft** the airplane will stall as soon as it gets airborne

> [!tip]+ Other Factors Affecting Performance
> **Altitude**
>
> - As altitude increases, outside PRESSURE DECREASES
>   - As a result, engine performance (propeller ) decreases
>   - lift is also reduced (fixed wing)
> - Think about flying at SEA LEVEL versus flying in Denver
>
> **Temperature**
> -As temperature INCREASES, performance DECREASES
> -As humidity INCREASES, performance DECREASES
> A Hot humid summer day = worst case scenario (way to go Florida)
>
> **Weight**
>
> - A HEAVIER aircraft requires more lift to get off the ground
> - More lift is achieved with FASTER SPEED (fixed weight)
> - More speed means MORE Drag which reduces performance
>   - Longer take off/landing distance
>   - Reduced climb performance
>
> **Additional Factors**
>
> - Runway/launch area length
> - Surface Type
> - Slope
> - Surface Wind

- Verifies "weight and balance" condition

## Axes of Rotation

#FAA/Definition

- **Roll** (longitudinal) — _ailerons_
- **Pitch** (lateral) — _elevators_
- **Yaw** (vertical) — _rudder_

## Forces of Flight

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110155115.png]]

Lift is from **WINGS (airfoil)** on fixed wing, rotating airfoils (rotor blades or propellers) on rotorcraft.

Applied at _Center of Pressure_ (also called Center of Lift).

**Counteracts weight.**

In straight-and-level, unaccelerated flight, lift and weight are **EQUAL**.

### Principles of LIFT

Angle of attack and relative wind interact with the flight path.

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110155642.png]]

Perpendicular to the relative wind is **LIFT**. #FAA/Definition

_Resultant Force_ = Lift vector plus drag vector.

Lift is affected by the **SPEED** of the airfoil through the air and the **ANGLE OF ATTACK** of the airfoil.

- Faster speed = more lift, all other factors being equal
- Higher angle of attack = more lift, up to the critical angle of attack

Lift can be explained using two complementary principles:

- **Newton's Third Law** — "For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction."

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110160248.png]]

- **Bernoulli's Principle** — "When a pipe is restricted, speed increases, pressure decreases."

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110155103.png]]

### Weight

Result of gravity.

Applied at _**CENTER OF GRAVITY**_.

Points to **CENTER OF THE EARTH**.

### THRUST

"Horizontal Lift" — similar aerodynamic principles; the propeller blades are airfoils.

Thrust is not necessarily horizontal, particularly in rotorcraft.

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261110162845.png]]

### DRAG

**Parasite Drag** — byproduct of flying through the air.

**INCREASES AS SPEED INCREASES**

**Parasite Drag — 3 types:** #FAA/Definition

- Form Drag — PT Cruiser vs. Ferrari
- Interference Drag — 1+1 = 3 (drag is greater than the individual parts)
- Skin Friction — sandpaper flies poorly

**Lift-Induced Drag:**

- Fixed wing — as soon as lift is created
- Rotorcraft — as soon as lift is created, including during hover

**Stall** — when an airfoil exceeds its critical angle of attack, airflow separates sufficiently to cause a significant reduction in lift. #FAA/Definition #FAA/Trap

A stall can occur at ANY airspeed if the critical angle of attack is exceeded.

## Load Factor

_Load Factor_ — the ratio of the aerodynamic load supported by the aircraft to the aircraft's weight. Generally expressed in G's. #FAA/Definition

**Load Factor = Lift ÷ Weight** #FAA/Formula

- Increases with **BANK ANGLE** (during a coordinated, level turn).

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261111110813.png]]

**THIS IS WHY THE BANK ANGLE IS IMPORTANT BECAUSE IT CAN ADD MULTIPLE G'S OF LOAD TO THE AIRCRAFT.** #FAA/Safety

_**STALL SPEED**_ INCREASES with load factor. #FAA/Trap

**ABRUPT MANEUVERS** attempted during **SLOWER AIRSPEED** can lead to stalling since they can increase the load factor, which increases the speed at which the aircraft is capable of stalling. #FAA/Safety

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261111112311.png]]

### Load Factor Calculations

For a coordinated, level turn:

- Load Factor = 1 ÷ cos(Bank Angle)
- Resultant Load = Aircraft Weight × Load Factor

#FAA/Formula

| Bank Angle | Load Factor |
|---|---|
| 0° | 1.00 G |
| 30° | 1.15 G |
| 45° | 1.41 G |
| 60° | 2.00 G |

## Factors Affecting Performance

- CG Location
- Altitude
- Temperature
- Weight

### CG Location

- _Center of Gravity_ — point at which WEIGHT is applied.
- _Center of Pressure_ — point at which the resultant aerodynamic force acts.
- _Arm (A)_ — the distance from a reference point (datum) to the location of an item's center of gravity.
- _Moment_ — the turning effect of a force about a reference point.

#FAA/Definition

**Moment = Weight × Arm** #FAA/Formula

![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261116123814.png]]

### Moving CG Location

#FAA/Exam #FAA/Safety #FAA/Practical

When an object is shifted from one location to another, it will affect CG Location.

Putting too much weight aft or forward could **EXCEED THE CG LIMITS**, which can result in dangerous aircraft attitudes and loss of control.

Always ensure equipment is loaded in a way that does not negatively affect the CG Location or cause it to exceed manufacturer limits.

### Exceeding CG Limits

> _Envelope_ — the allowable range of an aircraft's weight and CG location. #FAA/Definition

If you load an airplane in such a way that the CG is out of limits or **OUTSIDE THE ENVELOPE**, it won't perform properly. #FAA/Safety

- Too far **forward**: The airplane may be unable to raise its nose sufficiently for takeoff or landing.
- Too far **aft**: The airplane becomes less longitudinally stable, may stall shortly after takeoff, and may have difficulty or be incapable of recovering from a stall.

## Other Factors Affecting Performance

### Altitude

#FAA/Practical

- As altitude increases, outside **PRESSURE DECREASES**
- Air density decreases
- Propeller performance decreases
- Engine performance decreases in normally aspirated combustion engines
- Lift is also reduced, all other factors being equal
- Think about flying at SEA LEVEL versus flying in Denver

### Temperature

#FAA/Practical #FAA/Safety

- As temperature INCREASES, performance DECREASES
- As humidity INCREASES, performance DECREASES

A hot, humid summer day at high altitude = worst case scenario (way to go Florida).

### Weight

#FAA/Practical

- A HEAVIER aircraft requires more lift to get off the ground
- More lift can be achieved with FASTER SPEED (fixed wing)
- More speed means MORE Drag, which reduces performance
- Longer takeoff/landing distance
- Reduced climb performance
- Increased stall speed (fixed wing)

**Increasing weight increases stall speed but DOES NOT change the critical angle of attack for a given aircraft configuration.** #FAA/Trap

### Additional Factors

- Runway/launch area length
- Surface Type
- Slope
- Surface Wind

## Figures and Radio Pronunciation

> [!note]+ Standard Radio Pronunciation #FAA/Definition
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261122114312.png]]
>
> **NINER** (Nein means no in German)
>
> - **300:** Three Hundred
> - **4,500 feet:** Four Thousand FIFE hundred feet
> - **10,500 feet:** One Zero Thousand, FIFE hundred feet
> - All numbers are transmitted by _pronouncing each digit_, such as 18 — "One Eight"
> - _**Decimals**_ in frequencies are pronounced **POINT**.
>   - 122.1 — "One Two Two POINT One"

### Altitudes

> [!info]+ Altitude Pronunciation #FAA/Definition #FAA/Trap
> **Below 18,000 feet:**
>
> Separate **DIGITS** are used for thousands plus the hundreds.
>
> - **13,800 feet:** "One Three Thousand Eight Hundred"
>
> **At and ABOVE 18,000 feet:**
>
> State the words **FLIGHT LEVEL**.
>
> - **20,000 feet:** "Flight Level Two Zero Zero"

### Directions and Speeds

> [!tip]+ Directions and Airspeed #FAA/Practical
> **Directions** are always expressed with three digits and are magnetic.
>
> - **050°:** "Zero FIFE Zero"
>
> **Speeds** are expressed in knots, with each digit pronounced individually.
>
> - **320 knots:** "Three Two Zero Knots"

## Aircraft Call Signs

> [!note]+ Call Signs and Tail Numbers #FAA/Definition #FAA/Practical
> Each aircraft is assigned a **TAIL NUMBER** used during radio communications.
>
> On initial contact, the **FULL tail number** is used.
>
> - **N123AB:** "November One Two Three Alpha Bravo"
>
> The tail number may subsequently be shortened when permitted.
>
> - **Shortened:** "Three Alpha Bravo"

## Airports WITHOUT a Control Tower

### Position Reports

> [!warning]+ Non-Towered Airport Communications #FAA/Safety #FAA/Practical
>
> - When there is no tower, manned pilots **SHOULD** make radio calls on a common frequency to report their position.
> - These calls are **NOT always required**, so do not rely on hearing every aircraft.
>
> **Three ways to communicate intentions at non-towered airports:**
>
> 1. UNICOM Operator
> 2. Self-Announce Broadcast (CTAF)
> 3. Flight Service Station (FSS), if available at the field

### UNICOM

> [!info]+ UNICOM #FAA/Definition
>
> - Universal Integrated Community (as described in the course notes).
> - Provides airport information at a public-use airport, upon pilot request.
> - Pilots can request:
>   - Weather information
>   - Wind direction
>   - Traffic pattern information
> - Available when there is no tower or FSS available.

### CTAF — Common Traffic Advisory Frequency

> [!info]+ CTAF #FAA/Definition #FAA/Practical
> **CTAF** — Common Traffic Advisory Frequency.
>
> - The **MOST COMMON TYPE** of communications at an untowered airport.
> - Used to broadcast _position_ reports.
> - Can use any of the following:
>   - UNICOM/MULTICOM frequency
>   - Tower frequency
>   - FSS frequency (instructor said not to worry about FSS details)
>
> A few airports do not have a designated CTAF/UNICOM frequency.
>
> **MULTICOM frequency 122.9 MHz** is used at non-towered airports without an FSS or UNICOM.

## Non-Towered Airport Radio Examples

### Arriving at an Airport

> [!example]+ Example — Sedona Traffic #FAA/Practical

> [!danger]+ Remote Pilot Radio Communications #FAA/Safety
> Remote pilots should refrain from transmitting over active aviation frequencies unless there is an emergency situation.
>
> **Important for drone operations:** Listen for aircraft position reports and use the information to maintain awareness of nearby traffic. Do not assume that all aircraft will announce their position.

## Airports WITH a Control Tower

### Tower Communications

> [!note]+ Controlled Airport Communications #FAA/Practical
>
> - Manned pilots follow instructions from the **CONTROLLER**.
> - Pilots read back instructions when required.
> - Language is similar to non-towered airports:
>   - Entry
>   - Downwind
>   - Base
>   - Final

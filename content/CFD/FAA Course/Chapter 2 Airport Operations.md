> [!faq]+ Types of Airport #FAA/Definition
>
> - _Civil_ — open to the public.
> - _Military (Federal)_ — generally not open to the public.
> - _Private_ — requires permission from the owner.

> [!tip]+ Towered Airport #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam
>
> - Has an operating control tower.
> - Referred to as **ATC (Air Traffic Control)**.
> - ATC is responsible for the **safe, orderly, and expeditious flow of air traffic**.
> - Manned aircraft generally must establish communication with ATC when operating at a towered airport.

> [!info]+ Non-towered Airport #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Safety
>
> - **NO operating control tower.**
> - Typically found at smaller airports with lower traffic volumes.
> - No air traffic controller guiding pilots in.
> - Radio communication is generally **NOT required** at non-towered airports. This makes visual observation and the use of spotters particularly important for drone operations.
> - Pilots are encouraged to make radio calls using the designated frequency:
>   - **CTAF (Common Traffic Advisory Frequency)** — more likely to be encountered when using portable aviation radios.
>   - **UNICOM** — an aeronautical advisory station that may provide airport and traffic information.

> [!tldr]+ True vs. Magnetic North #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Formula #FAA/Trap
>
> We orient ourselves using two main references:
>
> - _True North_ — the geographic North Pole (Santa). Most maps are oriented in relation to True North.
> - _Magnetic North_ — the location toward which a magnetic compass points.
>   - **NOT the same as the geographic North Pole.**
>   - Magnetic North changes over time.
>   - Magnetic compasses and magnetic-heading instruments reference Magnetic North.
>
> ==COMPASS = Magnetic North | MAP = True North==
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118134057.png]]
>
> **Variation** — the angular difference between True North and Magnetic North, expressed in degrees and minutes.
>
> Variation changes depending on your **LOCATION** on Earth and is identified as **East** or **West**.
>
> To convert from True to Magnetic heading, add or subtract the variation.
>
> **Mnemonic:**
>
> ==East is LEAST, West is BEST.==
>
> - East variation → SUBTRACT.
> - West variation → ADD.
>
> **Isogonic Lines** — lines connecting locations of equal magnetic variation, shown on aeronautical charts.

> [!warning]+ Runway Orientation #FAA/Exam #FAA/Formula #FAA/Trap
>
> - Runways are identified by a two-digit number representing their approximate **MAGNETIC orientation**.
> - Runway identifiers can change over time as Magnetic North changes.
> - Based on 360 degrees, like a compass rose.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118135757.png]]
>
> **Runway numbers are pronounced as individual digits.**
>
> Eighteen becomes _"One Eight."_ This helps maintain clarity during radio interference.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118140046.png]]
>
> The runway number indicates the direction you are facing when using that runway. The airplane pictured is facing approximately North — Runway 36.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118140139.png]]
>
> The plane is taking off on approximately a **030° heading** (Runway 03).
>
> Runway numbers are abbreviations of actual magnetic headings.
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118140713.png]]
>
> **Remember:** Headings are expressed using THREE digits.
>
> - Runway 03 = approximately 030°.
> - Runway 18 = approximately 180°.
> - Runway 36 = approximately 360°.
>
> **FAA Exam Tricks**
>
> - Multiply the runway number by **10** to get its approximate magnetic heading.
> - Add or subtract **18** to determine the opposite runway number.
> - ==Runway 00 does NOT exist. North is Runway 36.==
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118140803.png]]
>
> With parallel runways, the opposite direction will have reversed left/right designations (duh).

> [!hint]+ Traffic Pattern #FAA/Exam #FAA/Safety #FAA/Practical
>
> - Important when operating near an airport.
> - Used by pilots to make an **organized approach to landing**.
> - Much of the typical traffic pattern is between the surface and approximately **1,000 feet AGL**.
>
> **AGL (Above Ground Level)** — height above the ground.
>
> **MSL (Mean Sea Level)** — altitude above average sea level.
>
> At non-towered airports, manned aircraft are encouraged to monitor and communicate on the CTAF within approximately **10 miles** of the airport when entering the traffic pattern.
>

> [!cite]+ Wind Direction Indicators #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Trap
>
> Three indicators:
>
> - **Windsock**
> - **Tetrahedron**
> - **Wind Tee**
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261118224204.png]]
>
> **FAA Exam Trap:**
>
> The narrow end of a windsock and the pointed end of a tetrahedron normally face opposite directions.
>
> - Windsock's narrow end points **DOWNWIND**.
> - Tetrahedron's pointed end indicates the intended landing direction.
>
> A tetrahedron is officially a **landing direction indicator**, not necessarily a reliable indication of actual wind direction.

> [!tldr]+ Airport Beacon #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Safety #FAA/Trap
>
> Helps identify the location of an airport at **NIGHT** or during **LOW VISIBILITY**.
>
> - Normally active from **SUNSET to SUNRISE**.
> - During daylight, at airports with controlled surface airspace, beacon operation may indicate weather below basic VFR minimums:
>   - Ground visibility less than **3 statute miles**.
>   - Cloud ceiling less than **1,000 feet**.
>
> **Do not rely solely on the beacon to determine actual weather conditions.**
>
> **Beacon Color Combinations:**
>
> | Airport Type | Beacon Colors |
> | --- | --- |
> | Civilian Land Airport | Green + White |
> | Military Airport | White + White + Green |
> | Water Airport | Yellow + Green |
> | Heliport | White + Yellow + Green |
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20261119111607.png]]
>
> **Part 107 Visibility Requirement:**
>
> Under 14 CFR § 107.51, the minimum flight visibility, as observed from the control station, is ==3 statute miles==.

> [!question]+ Operation Near / On Airports #FAA/Exam #FAA/Safety #FAA/Practical #FAA/Trap
>
> Operating near an airport does not, by itself, require FAA airspace authorization if you are in uncontrolled airspace.
>
> Authorization is required when operating in airspace subject to Part 107 airspace authorization requirements.
>
> ==DO NOT INTERFERE WITH AIRPORT OPERATIONS OR TRAFFIC PATTERNS.==
>
> Become familiar with:
>
> - Airport traffic patterns.
> - Aircraft arrival and departure directions.
> - Nearby manned aircraft activity.
>
> **Operation ON Airport Property**
>
> - Some commercial work may require you to operate on airport property.
> - Separate airport access permissions may apply.
> - Restricted security areas on airport property may include:
>   - **SIDA (Security Identification Display Area)**.
>   - Appropriate access credentials or badges are required for unescorted access.

> [!info]+ Other Considerations #FAA/Safety #FAA/Practical #FAA/TodoReview
>
> **Flying in the Wire Environment**
>
> - Wires may be practically **INVISIBLE** from the ground or through an onboard camera.
> - Judging **DEPTH** and **DISTANCE** is extremely difficult.
> - Striking a wire can damage or **DESTROY** your UAS and may cause damage to infrastructure.
> - Watch for **guy wires** on towers, particularly during inspections.
>
> ==ALWAYS perform a thorough PREFLIGHT inspection of the operating area to identify potential hazards.==
>
> Use **Sectional Charts** to help identify charted obstacles. Remember that not every wire or obstacle is depicted.
>
> **Laser Activity**
>
> Report hazardous laser activity to the FAA through the appropriate reporting channels.
>
> **Thermal Plumes**
>
> Avoid flying near:
>
> - Power plants.
> - Industrial production facilities.
> - Other industrial systems producing significant heat.
>
> Rising hot air can generate **turbulence** or reduce visibility. Excessive heat may also affect electronics and motors.
>
> **Bird Activity**
>
> Birds of prey may attack drones.
>
> Bird activity may also be reported in aviation notices (BIRDTAM).
>
> The instructor suggested vertical movement to evade a pursuing bird. Review this technique before relying on it; maintain safe clearance and comply with altitude restrictions.
>
> **Unmanned Balloons**
>
> Be aware of tethered balloons that may have nearly **INVISIBLE wires** holding them in position.

> [!done]+ Sources of Airport Data #FAA/Exam #FAA/Practical #FAA/Safety
>
> When operating near an airport, obtain as much relevant information as possible about the airport and surrounding airspace.
>
> Airport information can be found through several publications and services:
>
> - **Chart Supplements** — detailed airport information.
> - **NOTAMs (Notices to Airmen)** — time-sensitive updates (like a software patch).
> - **ATIS** — current airport and weather information.
> - **Aeronautical Charts**.

> [!bug]+ Chart Supplements
>
> **Chart Supplement (CS)** #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Practical
>
> - Provides detailed information about airports.
> - Formerly called **AFD (Airport/Facility Directory)**.
> - Course notes identify nine regional publications.
> - Can be downloaded for FREE as a PDF.
> - Updated every ==56 days==.
> - Always use the latest available edition.
>
> **Chart Supplement — Sections** #FAA/Exam #FAA/TodoReview
>
> - **Section 1 — Legend**
>   - Explains symbols, abbreviations, and formatting.
>   - Provided in the applicable FAA exam testing supplement.
> - **Section 2 — Airport/Facility Directory**
>   - Organized by state.
> - **Section 3 — Notices**
>   - Major changes and notices.
> - **Section 4 — Associated Data**
>   - Telephone numbers, frequencies, forms, etc.
> - **Section 5 — Airport Diagrams**
>   - Diagrams for applicable larger airports.
>

> [!tldr]+ **NOTAM (Notice to Airmen)** #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Safety
>
> Time-sensitive information concerning hazards, changes, or conditions that may affect aviation operations.
>
> Think of a NOTAM as a **software patch for aviation information** — an update issued between regularly scheduled publication cycles.
>
> The instructor describes the traditional NOTAM system as outdated.
>
> **Examples of NOTAM information:**
>
> - Airshows.
> - Parachute jumps.
> - Kite flying.
> - Rocket launches.
> - Flights involving important individuals, including heads of state.
> - Military exercises involving airspace restrictions.
> - Temporary obstacles near airports.
> - Bird activity affecting airspace (BIRDTAM).
> - Volcanic ash or other contamination (ASHTAM).
>
> _Course terminology to review: DROTAM — drone-related notices._

> [!hint]+ Types of NOTAMs
>
> **NOTAM Classifications** #FAA/Exam #FAA/Definition
>
> Focus primarily on the first two types:
>
> 1. **NOTAM(D)**
> 2. **FDC NOTAM**
> 3. Pointer NOTAM
> 4. Military NOTAM
>
> **NOTAM(D)** #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam
>
> Provides information concerning navigational facilities within the National Airspace System (NAS), including applicable public-use airports listed in Chart Supplements.
>
> Examples include:
>
> - Taxiway closures.
> - Personnel and equipment operating near runways.
> - Lighting aids not affecting instrument approaches.
>
> **FDC NOTAM** #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam #FAA/Trap
>
> - Issued by the **National Flight Data Center**.
> - Contains information affecting charts, procedures, and airspace usage.
> - Often relates to:
>   - IFR (Instrument Flight Rules) procedures.
>   - Temporary Flight Restrictions (TFRs).
>   - Other regulatory or procedural changes.
>
> ==CHECK ALL APPLICABLE NOTAMs BEFORE FLYING.==
>
> <a href="https://notams.aim.faa.gov/notamSearch/nsapp.html#/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FAA NOTAM Search</a>

> [!question]+ ATIS
>
> **ATIS (Automatic Terminal Information Service)** #FAA/Definition #FAA/Exam
>
> Provides continuously available recorded airport information, including **CURRENT LOCAL WEATHER**.
>
> - Typically updated **ONCE PER HOUR**.
> - May be updated more frequently when local conditions change.
> - Each recording receives a single-letter identifier, referred to as _Information_.
>
> Examples:
>
> - Information A — Alpha.
> - Information B — Bravo.
> - Information C — Charlie.
>
> **ATIS Weather Reporting Exception** #FAA/Exam #FAA/Trap
>
> The ceiling, visibility, and obstructions to vision may be omitted from the ATIS broadcast when BOTH of the following conditions are met:
>
> - Ceiling is ABOVE ==5,000 feet==.
> - Visibility is MORE THAN ==5 statute miles==.
